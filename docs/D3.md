# Software Engineering, Week 3: Requirements Package (D3)

**Author:** Eduardo Ion Bocos Varvari

**Project:** UoP Meeting Booking

This document describes the users of UoP Meeting Booking through personas and usage scenarios, and derives from them the user stories, business rules, acceptance criteria and priorities that make up the product backlog. The backlog itself is tracked in GitHub Issues: <https://github.com/bocoseduardo-dot/uop-meeting-booking/issues>.

## 1. Personas

Each persona represents a distinct category of user with its own goals and needs.

### Persona 1: Irene, Erasmus student

**Represents:** newly arrived exchange students. Unlike local students, they do not know how the University of the Peloponnese works, cannot read information that is only available in Greek, and do not yet know where professors' offices are.

**Context:** Several of her courses are taught in Greek, so she follows them through independent study with regular meetings with each professor. She therefore needs more meetings than a local student, with several professors at the same time. She organises them from her laptop at home and checks them on her phone between classes.

**Goals:**

- Schedule meetings with the professors of her courses.
- Know when and where each meeting takes place without having to email every professor.
  **Current frustrations:**
- Each professor arranges meetings differently (email, Google Calendar).
- She does not know where offices are, and some information is only in Greek.
- She has booked two meetings at the same time with different professors.
- She has no single place to see all her meetings.
  **Technical skills and devices:** Comfortable with web applications; uses a laptop and a smartphone.

**Needs from the product:**

- See when each professor is available.
- Book a meeting without exchanging emails.
- See all her meetings, with time and location, in one place.

### Persona 2: Alexandros, professor

**Represents:** faculty members who receive meeting requests from many students. He teaches three or four courses in the Department of Informatics and supervises final-year projects.

**Context:** In the first weeks of the semester his inbox fills up with meeting requests from Erasmus students, who follow his courses through independent study, and from newly arrived students, while he is also supervising other students' final projects. He works from his office computer, his laptop and his smartphone.

**Goals:**

- Let students see when he is available to meet.
- Let students know where to find him for each meeting (his office or online).
  **Current frustrations:**
- He receives many emails to arrange meetings while preparing his courses and projects.
- He has to update his calendar by hand after every request.
- He discovers overlapping meetings too late.
- When an unexpected commitment comes up, such as a conference abroad, he has to reschedule every affected meeting one by one by email.
  **Technical skills and devices:** Comfortable with web applications; uses his office computer, a laptop and a smartphone.

**Needs from the product:**

- Publish his availability once, with the location of each slot.
- Accept or decline requests depending on his schedule.
- See all his meetings in one place.
- Cancel or reschedule meetings when his plans change.
- Never end up with two meetings at the same time.

### Persona 3: Eleni, Student Affairs and Welfare officer

**Represents:** administrative staff of student support units. She is not a professor: she helps Erasmus students, newly arrived Greek students and students in later years with matters that are not about a course.

**Context:** At the start of the academic year, many students need her help at the same time: documents for state support such as student housing and scholarships, Erasmus paperwork such as learning agreements, and personal or academic difficulties. She works in an office with several colleagues and uses a shared office computer, a laptop and her phone.

**Goals:**

- Meet students in person, so she can talk to them face to face.
- Share the workload with her colleagues.
- Let students book a meeting without having to describe their personal situation in an email.
  **Current frustrations:**
- She receives many emails in both Greek and English.
- Some Erasmus students arrive without the documents they need, so the meeting has to be repeated.
- Long queues force students to come back several times.
  **Technical skills and devices:** Regular computer user without a technical background; uses a shared office computer, a laptop and a phone.

**Needs from the product:**

- Publish the available hours of the Student Affairs and Welfare office, not only her own.
- Tell students before the meeting which documents they must bring.
- Not store the reason for a meeting.

### Candidates considered and not used as personas

- **A local first-year student preparing for exams.** His goals and needs are the same as Irene's; what differs is the situation (high demand before exams). It is therefore modelled as Scenario 2, not as a separate persona.
- **University administration and the Quality Assurance Unit.** They are interested in aggregate statistics about the use of the system, but they do not book or offer meetings, and statistics are outside the current scope. They remain stakeholders (see D1), not personas.

## 2. Usage scenarios

### Scenario 1: Irene arranges her first meetings

**Persona:** Irene (Erasmus student)

In her first week, Irene needs to meet the three professors whose courses she will follow through independent study. She signs in to the meeting system with her university account and opens each professor's availability, which shows the date, time and location of every free slot. She requests a slot with each of the three professors without writing a single email. When she tries to request a fourth meeting, the system warns her that it overlaps with one she has already requested, so she chooses another time. Over the next two days, two professors accept her requests and one declines, so she books a different slot with him. At the end of the week she has all her meetings, with their times, locations and status, in one place, and knows exactly where to go.

| Fragment                                                         | Requirement                                                    | Type               |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| "Opens each professor's availability... date, time and location" | See free slots with date, time and location                    | User story         |
| "Requests a slot... without writing a single email"              | Request a meeting in a slot                                    | User story         |
| "The system warns her that it overlaps"                          | A student cannot have overlapping active meetings              | Business rule      |
| "Two professors accept... one declines"                          | The professor accepts or declines; the student sees the status | User story         |
| "Books a different slot"                                         | A declined request does not prevent new requests               | Business rule      |
| "All her meetings... in one place"                               | See all my meetings                                            | User story         |
| "Signs in with her university account"                           | Institutional authentication                                   | Assumption (later) |

### Scenario 2: Exam week rush

**Persona:** Alexandros (professor), with students from his Mathematics I course

A few days before the exam period, many first-year students want help with Mathematics I, the course Alexandros teaches. His free slots for the week are taken within a few hours. One student, Nikos, requests the last free slot at almost the same moment as another student; her request arrives first, so Nikos is told that the slot is no longer available. Looking at his schedule, Alexandros sees that all his slots are booked and that students are still looking for help, so he publishes several extra slots for the days before the exam. Nikos requests one of the new slots and Alexandros accepts it, so Nikos gets his tutoring without sending a single email.

| Fragment                                                                             | Requirement                                                                           | Type             |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------- |
| "His free slots... are taken within a few hours"                                     | Students only see free slots                                                          | User story       |
| "Requests the last free slot at almost the same moment... her request arrives first" | A slot has at most one active meeting; with simultaneous requests, the first one wins | Business rule    |
| "Nikos is told that the slot is no longer available"                                 | Clear error when a slot is no longer free                                             | Error handling   |
| "Sees that all his slots are booked"                                                 | The professor sees his slots with their status                                        | User story       |
| "Publishes several extra slots"                                                      | Publish several slots at once                                                         | User story (new) |
| "Alexandros accepts it"                                                              | Accept a request                                                                      | User story       |

### Scenario 3: Alexandros reschedules his week

**Persona:** Alexandros (professor)

Alexandros has accepted three meetings for next week when he is invited to an international conference on those same days. First, he removes his free slots for that week so that no more students can request them. One student still has a pending request, so he declines it. For each of the three accepted meetings, he proposes a new time in the following week, and the students are notified of the change. Two of them accept the new time, and their meetings are updated. The third student cannot attend at the proposed time, so she declines the change; the meeting is cancelled and she books another of his slots later. Everything is solved without a single email.

| Fragment                                                          | Requirement                                                                     | Type          |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------- |
| "He removes his free slots for that week"                         | A professor can remove his free slots                                           | User story    |
| "One student still has a pending request, so he declines it"      | A professor can decline a pending request                                       | User story    |
| "He proposes a new time"                                          | A professor can propose a new time for an accepted meeting                      | User story    |
| (implicit)                                                        | A proposed new time cannot overlap with another active meeting of the professor | Business rule |
| "The students are notified of the change"                         | Students are notified when a meeting changes                                    | User story    |
| "Two of them accept the new time, and their meetings are updated" | A student can accept or decline a proposed change                               | User story    |
| "She declines the change; the meeting is cancelled"               | Declining a proposed change cancels the meeting                                 | Business rule |
| "She books another of his slots later"                            | A cancelled meeting no longer counts as active, so the student can book again   | Business rule |

### Scenario 4: A confidential appointment

**Persona:** Eleni (Student Affairs and Welfare officer), with a student

Eleni publishes the office's available slots for the coming weeks, which are covered by the officers on rotation. A student who needs guidance about a personal situation books one of these slots; he only chooses a time and does not have to write the reason anywhere. The booking is confirmed, together with a list of the documents he must bring, such as his ID card. On the office's shared schedule, every officer on rotation can see that the student has an appointment at that time, but not why. The reason is only discussed in person when the student arrives at the office.

| Fragment                                                                      | Requirement                                                                                           | Type          |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------- |
| "Eleni publishes the office's available slots"                                | An officer can publish slots on behalf of the office                                                  | User story    |
| "Covered by the officers on rotation"                                         | Slots belong to the office, so any of its officers can attend; each officer has an individual account | Business rule |
| "He only chooses a time and does not have to write the reason"                | The system never asks for or stores the reason for a meeting                                          | Business rule |
| "Together with a list of the documents he must bring"                         | An officer can state, when publishing a slot, which documents the student must bring                  | User story    |
| "Every officer... can see that the student has an appointment... but not why" | Office staff can see who has an appointment and when, but never the reason                            | Business rule |

## 3. User stories and business rules

Requirements are classified into two types. A **user story** describes something a person can do with the system. A **business rule** describes something the system always guarantees, whoever performs the action. Each business rule is linked to the story it protects.

### User stories

| ID   | Story                                                                                                                                              | Source                        |
| ---- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| US1  | As a professor, I want to publish time slots with their location, so that students know when and where I am available.                             | Alexandros, Scenario 1        |
| US2  | As a student, I want to see professors' free slots with date, time and location, so that I can choose a meeting without emailing.                  | Irene, Scenarios 1 and 2      |
| US3  | As a student, I want to request a meeting in a free slot, so that I do not have to arrange it by email.                                            | Irene, Scenario 1             |
| US4  | As a professor, I want to accept or decline meeting requests, so that I only commit to meetings that fit my schedule.                              | Alexandros, Scenarios 1 and 3 |
| US5  | As a student, I want to see all my meetings with their time, location and status, so that I know where to go and what is confirmed.                | Irene, Scenario 1             |
| US6  | As a professor, I want to see my slots and meetings with their status, so that I can manage my week in one place.                                  | Alexandros, Scenario 2        |
| US7  | As a professor, I want to publish several slots at once, so that I can respond quickly to high demand, such as before exams.                       | Scenario 2                    |
| US8  | As a professor, I want to remove my free slots, so that students cannot request times when I am unavailable.                                       | Scenario 3                    |
| US9  | As a professor, I want to propose a new time for an accepted meeting, so that I can reschedule without emails.                                     | Scenario 3                    |
| US10 | As a student, I want to accept or decline a proposed new time, so that my meetings fit my availability.                                            | Scenario 3                    |
| US11 | As a student or professor, I want to cancel a meeting, so that the slot becomes free again.                                                        | Scenario 3                    |
| US12 | As a user, I want to be notified when one of my meetings is requested, accepted, declined, changed or cancelled, so that I do not miss any change. | Scenario 3                    |

| US14 | As a Student Affairs officer, I want to state which documents a student must bring when I publish a slot, so that meetings are not wasted.         | Eleni, Scenario 4             |
| US15 | As a user, I want to sign in with my university account, so that I do not need a separate password.                                                | Scenario 1                    |
| US16 | As a user, I want to identify myself with my own account, so that the system can show my meetings and act on my behalf.                            | Required by US5               |

### Business rules

| ID   | Rule                                                                                                                         | Protects       |
| ---- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------- |
| BR1  | A slot's end must be after its start.                                                                                        | US1            |
| BR2  | A slot can have at most one active meeting (requested or accepted); with simultaneous requests, the first one wins.          | US3            |
| BR3  | A student cannot have overlapping active meetings.                                                                           | US3            |
| BR4  | A professor cannot publish overlapping slots.                                                                                | US1, US7       |
| BR5  | A proposed new time cannot overlap with another active meeting of the professor.                                             | US9            |
| BR6  | Declining a proposed change cancels the meeting.                                                                             | US10           |
| BR7  | Cancelled and declined meetings are kept as history but no longer count as active.                                           | US3, US4, US11 |
| BR8  | The system never asks for or stores the reason for a meeting.                                                                | US3            |
| BR9  | Office slots belong to the office; each officer has an individual account.                                                   | US13           |
| BR10 | Meeting details are visible only to the people involved; office staff see who has an appointment and when, never the reason. | US5, US13      |
| BR11 | Slots whose start time has already passed are not offered to students.                                                       | US2            |

## 4. Acceptance criteria

Acceptance criteria are written for the Must stories in the form Given / When / Then. They cover the state of the system, the behaviour of the API and errors that cannot occur through the web page but can be triggered by a crafted request. Conflicts with the current state of the system return 409; invalid data returns 422.

**US1. Publish slots** (implemented in v0.1.0)

- Given an existing professor, when a slot with a valid start, end and location is published, then the API returns 201 with the slot, including its id and professor, and the slot is stored in the database.
- Given a slot whose end is not after its start, when it is published, then the API returns 422 and nothing is stored.
- Given an empty location, or one longer than 100 characters, when the slot is published, then the API returns 422.
- Given a professor id that does not exist, when a slot is published for it, then the API returns 422 and nothing is stored.
  **US2. See free slots**

- Given published slots, some of them with an active meeting, when a student lists the available slots, then the API returns 200 with only the free slots, ordered by start time.
- Each slot in the response includes its start, end, location and the professor's id and name, but not the professor's email.
- Given that no slot is free, when a student lists the available slots, then the API returns 200 with an empty list.
- Given a free slot whose start time has already passed, when a student lists the available slots, then it is not included.
  **US3. Request a meeting**

- Given an identified student and a free slot, when she requests it, then a meeting linked to her and to the slot is stored with status "requested", the API returns 201 with the meeting, and the slot no longer appears as free.
- Given a slot that already has an active meeting, when another student requests it, then the API returns 409 and no meeting is stored.
- Given two requests for the same free slot at almost the same time, when both are processed, then only the first one creates a meeting and the second one receives 409.
- Given a student with an active meeting, when she requests a slot that overlaps with it, then the API returns 409 and no meeting is stored.
- Given a slot id that does not exist, when it is requested, then the API returns 422 and no meeting is stored.
  **US4. Accept or decline a request**

- Given a meeting in status "requested" for one of his slots, when the professor accepts it, then its status is stored as "accepted" and the API returns 200 with the updated meeting.
- Given a meeting in status "requested", when the professor declines it, then its status is stored as "declined", the meeting is kept as history, and the slot appears as free again.
- Given a meeting that is not in status "requested", when the professor tries to accept or decline it, then the API returns 409 and the meeting does not change.
- Given a meeting for another professor's slot, when a professor tries to accept or decline it, then the API returns 404 and the meeting does not change. A 404 is used instead of a 403 so that the existence of other people's meetings is not revealed.
  **US5. See my meetings**

- Given an identified student with meetings in different statuses, when she lists her meetings, then the API returns 200 with all of them, each with its time, location, professor's name and status.
- Given meetings of other students, when she lists her meetings, then none of them is included.
- Given a request without identification, when it asks for "my meetings", then the API returns 401.
  **US16. Identify myself**

- Given a registered user with valid credentials, when they sign in, then they are identified for their following requests.
- Given wrong credentials, when someone tries to sign in, then the API returns 401 without saying whether the email or the password was wrong.
- Given a request without identification to an endpoint that requires it, when it is processed, then the API returns 401.
- Given any stored account, its password is never stored in plain text, only as a secure hash.

## 5. Prioritisation (MoSCoW)

Must stories are those without which the product does not solve the problem stated in D1. They are kept well below 60% of the estimated effort, so that the prioritisation leaves real room for change. Business rules take the priority of the story they protect.

| ID   | Priority          | Justification                                                                                                            |
| ---- | ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| US1  | Must              | Without published slots there is nothing to book; it is the reason the product exists.                                   |
| US2  | Must              | Students must see what professors publish in order to choose a meeting.                                                  |
| US3  | Must              | Requesting a meeting is the core action that replaces email.                                                             |
| US4  | Must              | Without accepting or declining, a request never becomes a meeting.                                                       |
| US5  | Must              | D1 states that students have no record of when and where their meetings take place; this story solves exactly that.      |
| US16 | Must              | US5 needs to know who the user is. A simple identification is required until institutional login is possible.            |
| US6  | Should            | Important for professors, but they can use the list of their meetings in the meantime.                                   |
| US7  | Should            | Useful at peaks of demand; until then, slots can be published one by one.                                                |
| US8  | Should            | Without it, the professor can still decline requests for times when he is unavailable.                                   |
| US11 | Should            | Needed to undo meetings; it is also the simple alternative to rescheduling.                                              |
| US9  | Should            | Rescheduling can be replaced by cancelling and booking again, and it is one of the most complex features.                |
| US10 | Should            | Depends on US9.                                                                                                          |
| US12 | Could             | Valuable, but it depends on access to the university's email service, which is not confirmed.                            |
| US13 | Won't (this time) | Introduces a new type of user, new permissions and slots owned by units; D1 limits the scope to students and professors. |
| US14 | Won't (this time) | Depends on US13.                                                                                                         |
| US15 | Won't (this time) | Depends on institutional authentication facilities that still have to be verified with the IT unit.                      |
