# Software Engineering, Week 1: Product Vision (D1)

**Author:** Eduardo Ion Bocos Varvari

**Project:** UoP Meeting Booking (working title)

UoP Meeting Booking is a system for booking meetings between students and professors, designed for the Department of Informatics at the University of the Peloponnese (UoP) in Tripoli. It addresses a real problem I have experienced as an exchange student: there is no common way to arrange meetings with professors.

## 1. Problem Statement

Based on my experience as an exchange student, the University of the Peloponnese (UoP) does not have software that allows students to book meetings with professors. Meetings are currently arranged through Google Calendar and email, but each professor uses their own Google Calendar or checks their email differently. As a result, students have to adapt to each professor in order to get a meeting, and they have no persistent record of where and when their meetings take place.

For professors, this means having to check their own calendar manually every time they need to offer a time slot to a student who requests a meeting.

In practice, this leads to students having overlapping meetings with different professors, last-minute schedule changes, and incorrect or missing information about when and where meetings take place.

## 2. Intended Users

- **Student.** Wants to see all their meetings in one place and book new ones without having to look up a professor's email or access their Google Calendar. Today, they must adapt to each professor's way of working and have no single record of their meetings.
- **Professor.** Wants to publish when they are available to meet, see all their meetings in one place, and accept or decline meeting requests depending on their schedule.

## 3. Stakeholder Identification

- **UoP (institution):** cares about the protection of its members' personal data and the proper use of institutional accounts; constrains the product by requiring that the personal data of students and professors is handled responsibly.
- **Secretariat:** cares about the recurring questions it receives from students about where and when professors are available; constrains the product because, if the system were extended to class timetables, the secretariat would become a new user responsible for maintaining them.
- **Developer (me):** cares about delivering good software within the time available; constrains the product because, with only one semester, features and scalability are limited, and any goal that is not reached will be recorded explicitly as a non-goal.

## 4. Proposed Value

Unlike email and individual Google Calendars, this product shows students when each professor is available to meet and prevents them from booking meetings that overlap with their other meetings, so they no longer need to exchange repeated emails to find a free slot. Professors can see their meetings in the same place where they accept or decline requests.

In this document, a professor's *availability* is made up of *slots*: time periods with a date, a start and end time, and a location.

## 5. Principal Capabilities

1. A student can view each professor's availability and request a meeting within a slot.
2. A student can see all their meetings in one place.
3. A student is prevented from creating overlapping meetings.
4. A student can cancel a meeting.
5. A student can accept or decline a change proposed by a professor; declining cancels the meeting, accepting updates it to the new time.
6. A professor can publish their availability as slots, including the location of each slot.
7. A professor can accept or decline meeting requests.
8. A professor can see all their meetings in one place.
9. A professor can propose a new time for an existing meeting, provided it does not overlap with another meeting.
10. A professor can cancel an accepted meeting.
11. Users receive an email notification when a meeting is requested, accepted, declined, changed or cancelled.

## 6. Assumptions and Constraints

### Assumptions

- Professors are willing to publish their availability for meetings.
- All students and professors have a UoP email account that can be used to identify them.
- Based on my experience as an exchange student, no common booking tool currently exists at UoP.
- Students will prefer a single place to manage their meetings over using separate emails and calendars.
- Class timetables are already available to students through e-class.
- The official name of the department is the Department of Informatics (to be verified).

### Constraints

- The product is developed by a single person as a learning project.
- The product must be completed within one semester, so priority is given to a working core over additional features or polish.
- The course recommends that the product includes a persistent data store, automated tests and deployment to a realistic environment, and I intend to include all three.
- The system is designed for the scale of a single department, not the whole university.

## 7. Initial Scope

The first version targets students and professors of the Department of Informatics at UoP in Tripoli. It includes the full meeting cycle (publishing availability, requesting, accepting or declining, changing and cancelling meetings, with overlap prevention) exposed through a REST API and a minimal web interface. Email notifications inform users of changes, but the booking process continues to work even if the email service is unavailable.

## 8. Non-Goals

- **Secretariat role:** the secretariat will not have a role in this version, because each professor manages their own availability. If course timetables are added in the future, this decision can be reconsidered.
- **Room booking:** the product makes booking meetings easier but will not manage rooms. The professor enters the location of each slot as free text, and the system only displays it.
- **Class timetables:** the product will not include class timetables, which are already published through e-class as far as I know.
- **Google Calendar integration:** the product will not integrate with Google Calendar, because I do not know what permissions UoP email accounts allow.
